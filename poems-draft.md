- **The Passionate Shepherd to His Love** — “Come live with me, and be my love; / And we will all the pleasures prove / That hills and valleys, dales and fields, / Woods, or steepy mountain yields.”
- **A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning** — “As virtuous men passe mildly away, / And whisper to their soules, to goe, / Whilst some of their sad friends doe say, / The breath goes now, and some say, no:”
- **To the Virgins, to Make Much of Time** — “Gather ye Rose-buds while ye may, / Old Time is still a-flying: / And this same flower that smiles to day, / To morrow will be dying.”

## Early 20th century (18)

- **The Lake Isle of Innisfree** — “I will arise and go now, and go to Innisfree, / And a small cabin build there, of clay and wattles made; / Nine bean rows will I have there, a hive for the honey bee, / And live alone in the bee-loud glade.”
- **The Stolen Child** — “Come away, O human child! / To the waters and the wild / With a faery, hand in hand, / For the world's more full of weeping than you can understand.”
- **He Wishes for the Cloths of Heaven** (page: Aedh Wishes for the Cloths of Heaven) — “Had I the heavens' embroidered cloths, / Enwrought with golden and silver light, / The blue and the dim and the dark cloths / Of night and light and the half light, / …”
- **The Wild Swans at Coole (poem)** — “The trees are in their autumn beauty, / The woodland paths are dry, / Under the October twilight the water / Mirrors a still sky; / …”
- **Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening** — “Whose woods these are I think I know. / His house is in the village though; / He will not see me stopping here / To watch his woods fill up with snow.”
- **Nothing Gold Can Stay (poem)** — “Nature's first green is gold, / Her hardest hue to hold. / Her early leaf's a flower; / But only so an hour. / …”
- **Fire and Ice (poem)** — “Some say the world will end in fire, / Some say in ice. / From what I’ve tasted of desire / I hold with those who favor fire. / …”  ✂︎ *end of the world*
- **O Captain! My Captain!** — “O Captain! My Captain! our fearful trip is done; / The ship has weather'd every rack, the prize we sought is won; / The port is near, the bells I hear, the people all exulting, / While follow eyes the steady keel, the vessel grim and daring: / …”  ✂︎ *a death*
- **A Noiseless Patient Spider** — “A noiseless, patient spider, / I mark'd, where, on a little promontory; it stood, isolated; / Mark'd how, to explore the vacant, vast surrounding, / It launch'd forth filament, filament, filament, out of itself; / …”
- **The Red Wheelbarrow** — “so much depends / upon”
- **Fog (poem)** — “The fog comes / on little cat feet.”
- **Trees (poem)** — “I think that I shall never see / A poem lovely as a tree.”
- **There Will Come Soft Rains (poem)** — “"There Will Come Soft Rains" / (War Time)”  ✂︎ *war poem*
- **First Fig** (page: Edna St. Vincent Millay) — “Those hours when happy hours were my estate, — / Entailed, as proper, for the next in line, / Yet mine the harvest, and the title mine — / Those acres, fertile, and the furrows straight, / …”
- **Anecdote of the Jar** — “I placed a jar in Tennessee, / And round it was, upon a hill. / It made the slovenly wilderness / Surround that hill.”
- **The Snow Man** — “One must have a mind of winter / To regard the frost and the boughs / Of the pine-trees crusted with snow;”
- **Harlem (poem)** — “What happens to a dream deferred? / Does it dry up / like a raisin in the sun? / Or fester like a sore— / …”  ✂︎ *"rotten meat"; heavier tone*
- **The Negro Speaks of Rivers** — “I've known rivers: / I've known rivers ancient as the world and older than the / flow of human blood in human veins.”

## Translated & world (12)

- **Quiet Night Thought** — “My casement veils glowing pools of moonbeams, / Perhaps on the ground is simply frost it seems; / Lifting my head I gaze up at the gleaming moon, / Bowing my head I ponder my homesick dreams.”  ✂︎ *Wikipedia quotes it in pinyin, English may be missing*
- **Classic of Poetry** — “The fishhawks sing gwan-gwan / On sandbars of the stream. / Gentle maiden, pure and fair, / Fit pair for a prince.”
- **Old pond** (page: Haiku) — “old pond / frog leaps in / water's sound”
- **Oku no Hosomichi** — “kusa no to mo / sumikawaru yo zo / hina no ie”  ✂︎ *romanized Japanese*
- **Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam** — “No. 149 I desire a little ruby wine and a book of verses, / Just enough to keep me alive, and half a loaf is needful; / And then, that I and thou should sit in a desolate place / Is better than the kingdom of a sultan.”
- **Le Lac (poem)** — “So driven onward to new shores forever, / Into the night eternal swept away, / Upon the sea of time can we not ever / Drop anchor for one day?”
- **L'albatros (poem)** — “Often, to amuse themselves, the crewmen / Catch albatrosses, vast sea-birds, / Which follow, indolent companions of the voyage, / The ship gliding on the bitter gulfs.”
- **Der Erlkönig** — “Who rides, so late, through night and wind? / It is the father with his child. / He has the boy well in his arm, / He holds him safely, he keeps him warm.”  ✂︎ *the child dies*
- **Ode to Joy** — “Joy, thou beauteous godly lightning, / Daughter of Elysium, / Fire drunken we are ent'ring / Heavenly, thy holy home! / …”  ✂︎ *mostly German on the page*
- **Sonnets to Orpheus** — “A God is able. But tell me, how shall / a man follow him through the narrow lyre? / His mind is divided. At the crossing of two / heart roads there is no temple for Apollo.”  ✂︎ *dense in translation*
- **Ithaca (poem)** — “Sa vgeis ston pigaimó gia tin Itháki, / na éfchesai nánai makrýs o drómos, / gemátos peripéteies, gemátos gnóseis. / Tous Laistrygónas kai tous Kýklopas, / …”  ✂︎ *mostly Greek on the page*
- **Carpe diem** — “Tu ne quaesieris, scire nefas, quem mihi, quem tibi / finem di dederint, Leuconoe, nec Babylonios / temptaris numeros. ut melius, quidquid erit, pati. / seu pluris hiemes seu tribuit Iuppiter ultimam, / …”  ✂︎ *mostly Latin on the page*
